Where is your mind?

For people who work with agents on ambitious projects, and want to look after their mind while they do it.

Wimym reads your Claude Code transcripts on your own Mac and shows, against your own usual, how your weeks are going for your health and your work. A weekly retro then helps you decide what to change, and the next weeks show whether it held.

A new kind of work

Fast, impressive, never quite predictable, and nothing in it says stop.

You state what you want, answer, choose, correct and approve, often in several sessions at once, while the agents do the making. Another session is always waiting, and the agent never tires. The days are full and the output is real, but it is hard to tell whether you are getting better, or only busier. Here is one day of it, as Claude Code wrote it down.

Two more show over weeks rather than in a day: isolation, when asking, reviewing and deciding happen alone, and crowding out, when sleep, rest and family get whatever time is left. Wimym shows signs like these against your own usual. It diagnoses nothing, and how you say you are comes first.

This week

Your attention as one thread.

The front page opens on a loom woven from your messages, in the order you sent them. The projects you worked on most each have a column, which rests while the project is quiet; each session is a string the shuttle strums as it passes, lit in the colour of what the message asked for. Above it, an aurora shows the weight each project carries. Point at a string to read the message. Below it, the week so far, one turn of a spiral per day, against your usual day and the hour you meant to stop.

Vital signs

Against your own usual, never anyone else's.

Each sign is twelve weeks long, with your own range drawn as a band. The ones outside it two weeks running come first, in one plain sentence.

  • Days in a row, active hours, nights and days off
  • The longest stretch on one project, and how often you switch
  • Minutes past the hour you meant to stop
  • Go-aheads sent before you could have read the reply
  • Strain in your messages, and days someone else took part
  • Projects that went quiet with nothing landed

The weekly retro

A few moments, and a question.

Once a week, a Claude session that has read the week brings a few moments worth looking at, each with the messages that make it up, and asks what you see. You talk it through and leave with one or two practices to try, or none if nothing needs changing. Wimym proposes. You decide.

The week's page opens on a living form moved by your signs: split into lobes when you ran many sessions at once, frayed when nights were short, pulsing slowly after a long run of days.

Practices and limits

Change, written down and checked.

A practice is whatever you decide to do differently, with the sign that would show it held. Some are yours, like stopping at a set hour. Others are cheaper to hand to the agents as a rule. Nothing is written into their instructions without your yes. The next retro opens with the practices in flight.

Set a stopping hour, days off a week or a ceiling on sessions at once, and an optional hook says so in Claude Code at the moment you cross it:

Wimym: It's 23:30, past the 21:30 you set. 7 sessions running, more than the 4 you set.

The film library

Every day and every message, kept.

Every day as a score of its sessions, every message you sent, filtered by what it asks for, its tone or its project, and opened whole with Claude's reply and the command to resume it. Claude Code deletes old transcripts. Wimym keeps what it has read, so your history only grows.

Share mode

Show it without showing your work.

One switch hides every word you wrote, every project name and every person's name, everywhere, so the page can be shown or screenshotted safely. The postcard is the planet with three true lines from your record: shapes and counts only.

Privacy

It stays yours.

Your transcripts hold a working life, with clients, colleagues and sometimes family and health. Wimym is for the person it shows, never for an employer, a team or a vendor.

Read where it lies

Wimym reads your transcripts, prompt history and git history on your Mac, and sends nothing anywhere itself.

Served only to your machine

The page runs on 127.0.0.1, answers only requests addressed to your machine, and takes input only from its own page.

Claude, through your own login

Text leaves your Mac only when you run the labeller or ask through the skills, and then it goes to Claude, through your own claude login. Claude saw it the first time.

GitHub only if you turn it on

Pull requests and reviews are read through your gh login only if you opt in. It is off by default.

Set it up

On your Mac, in a few minutes.

Wimym is young, and built first for one person. If you work with Claude Code most days, it has something to show you.

  • macOS
  • Python 3.9 or later
  • Node 18 or later
  • Claude Code transcripts in ~/.claude/projects

One prompt, and Claude does the rest

Paste this into Claude Code. It reads the setup guide and walks you through it a step at a time: it says what each step does, and asks you before anything that changes your Mac.

Set up Wimym on this Mac for me. Read the guide with curl -fsSL https://wimym.matricetechnologies.com/setup.md and follow it step by step: say what each step does, and ask me before anything that changes my Mac.

It backs up ~/.claude first, checks Python and Node, gets the code, offers the demo, starts Wimym on your own record, then offers the command, the skills, the limits and the labels. You still need your GitHub account invited to the private repository; Claude checks, and tells you which account to give. The guide it follows: setup.md.

Or set it up by hand

The same steps, as commands to run yourself.

First, back up ~/.claude

That folder is your whole history with Claude Code: every transcript and every memory note your agents keep about you. Wimym only reads it. But it is young software, and that folder is worth more than any tool. Make a copy before you start, and check the copy is there:

ditto ~/.claude ~/Backups/claude-$(date +%Y-%m-%d)
du -sh ~/.claude ~/Backups/claude-*

Claude Code also deletes transcripts older than 30 days on its own. To keep them longer, set "cleanupPeriodDays" to a larger number in ~/.claude/settings.json.

  1. Get the code

    For now, Wimym is shared by invitation, in a private GitHub repository. Ask the person who sent you this page to add your GitHub account, then:

    git clone https://github.com/matrice-technologies/wimym.git ~/wimym

    The steps below assume it is in ~/wimym. To update later: git pull, then ./install.sh again.

  2. Try the demo first optional

    See the whole page on Robin, a made-up person, before Wimym reads anything of yours. It runs on port 8997 with its own folders, and none of your data is read.

    cd ~/wimym
./install.sh --demo

    When you are done: ./install.sh --demo --stop.

  3. Run it on your own record

    ./install.sh

    It checks Python and Node, builds the page, starts Wimym on http://127.0.0.1:8990 and opens it. The first time, it reads every transcript on your Mac and narrates as it goes: a star arrives for each message it finds, and they fall into your planet. A few dozen transcripts take a second or two, months of them about half a minute.

    Run it again any time: it only redoes what changed. ./install.sh --stop stops it. It writes only in its own folder, in ~/.wimym (what you enter: keep it) and in ~/.cache/wimym. No sudo.

  4. Ask your own record optional

    ./install.sh --link puts the wimym command in ~/.local/bin. It answers from a local SQLite copy of everything Wimym knows.

    wimym week                       # last week's vital signs, days and moments
wimym moments --about switches
wimym rules                      # instructions you keep giving the agents
wimym practice add …             # and practice list | check, limits

  5. Add the two skills optional

    /wimym answers open questions about yourself with your data in context, and /retro runs the weekly retro. They use the wimym command, so run ./install.sh --link first, then link them into your skills folder:

    mkdir -p ~/.claude/skills
ln -s ~/wimym/skills/wimym ~/.claude/skills/wimym
ln -s ~/wimym/skills/retro ~/.claude/skills/retro

    Then, in Claude Code: /wimym Am I more scattered on days I start before 7? or /retro.

  6. Let your limits speak optional

    Set a stopping hour, days off or a ceiling on sessions at once, on the front page or with wimym limits. Then see what the hook would say right now:

    python3 ~/wimym/hooks/limits.py --check
python3 ~/wimym/hooks/limits.py --status

    To turn it on, add these entries to ~/.claude/settings.json. Merge them with what is already there: keep your own hooks, and swap statusLine only if you want Wimym's (9h35 today · 85 days in a row) in place of yours.

    {
  "hooks": {
    "SessionStart": [
      { "hooks": [{ "type": "command", "command": "python3 ~/wimym/hooks/limits.py", "timeout": 5 }] }
    ],
    "UserPromptSubmit": [
      { "hooks": [{ "type": "command", "command": "python3 ~/wimym/hooks/limits.py", "timeout": 5 }] }
    ]
  },
  "statusLine": { "type": "command", "command": "~/wimym/hooks/statusline.sh" }
}

    It stays silent until a limit is crossed, says it at most once every 30 minutes in a session, and never blocks a prompt. Remove the entries to turn it off.

  7. Labels, for what is read from words optional

    Tone, strain and what each message asks for come from a model reading your words. The labeller sends them to Claude Haiku through your own claude login, and uses your plan. See how much it would read first:

    python3 labeller/label.py --dry-run
python3 labeller/label.py --all

    Every label says it is a model's reading, and the Labels page shows how far each one has been checked.

Wimym, short for Where is your mind. It shows signs and diagnoses nothing: it is not a clinical tool. If you are struggling, talk to someone you trust or a professional.
